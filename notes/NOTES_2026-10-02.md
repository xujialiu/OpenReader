## The phone's colour picker answers upper-case `#RRGGBBAA`, and a whole percent survives the round trip (2026-10-02 10:07)

Read from `@expo/ui` 57.0.19's `node_modules/@expo/ui/ios/ColorPickerView.swift`, not measured on a device. `colorToHex` takes `UIColor(color).cgColor.components`, clamps each to 0–1, scales by 255, rounds, and formats `#%02X%02X%02X%02X` when `supportsOpacity` is on (`#%02X%02X%02X` when off). So a colour outside sRGB, picked from the phone's Display P3 spectrum, is clamped channel by channel. The red, green and blue are `components[0]`, `components[1]` if there are more than one, and `components[2]` if there are more than two. A grey whose `CGColor` is in a grey colour space has two components, white and alpha, so this would answer its alpha as the green channel. Whether the system picker ever hands one back is not known. `supportsOpacity` defaults to `true` on the Swift side (`@Field var supportsOpacity: Bool = true`). An answer equal to `previousHex` is not sent, and a new `selection` from JS resets `previousHex` first, so feeding the picker back what it answered does not echo.

`highlight-colours.ts`'s `toPicker` writes `round(percent × 2.55)` as the alpha byte, and `fromPicker` reads `round(byte / 255 × 100)`. In node, every percent from 0 to 100 comes back as itself. Of the 256 alpha bytes the picker can answer, 155 come back one byte off as the percent's own byte, never by more than 1. After that one step every byte is stable. 62 % is `9e` and 22 % is `38`.
