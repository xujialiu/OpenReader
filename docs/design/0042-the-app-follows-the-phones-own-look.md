# The app follows the phone's own look

## What was decided

Wherever the phone has its own way of drawing something, such as a page of
settings, a switch, a menu, a typed field, a button or a panel that rises from
the bottom, OpenReader draws it the phone's way, as closely as it can. The owner
judges the app's pages against the phone's own apps, above all its Settings, and
not against other reading apps or against a look of the app's own. When a page
could be drawn two ways, the one nearer the phone wins unless it costs something
listed below.

"As closely as it can" has an order:

1. **The phone draws it.** Where the phone will draw a control for the app and
   that control does the job, the app lets it. The short menus for Theme and
   Alignment are the phone's own, and so are the switches and the bubble the
   speed opens in.
2. **The app draws it the phone's way.** Where letting the phone draw it would
   cost too much, or would break something the app promises, the app draws its
   own copy with the phone's shapes, sizes, spacing and behaviour, measured from
   the phone itself rather than remembered. The settings pages are drawn this
   way.
3. **The app's own design, in the phone's idiom.** Only where the phone has no
   convention at all does the app design something of its own: the reading page,
   the moving highlight, and the controls that step by sentence and paragraph.
   Even then it uses the phone's shapes: round buttons, panels that rise from
   the bottom and close by dragging, and cards of rows.

## Why

- **Nothing to learn.** The owner already knows how the phone's switches, menus
  and settings pages behave. A control that looks like the phone's is understood
  before it is tapped.
- **One standard, decided in advance.** The settings pages ended up drawn four
  different ways because each was designed on its own (decision 0041). The
  phone's conventions give every new page an answer before anyone designs it,
  and a way to tell whether two pages match.
- **It keeps up with the phone.** What the phone draws itself changes when the
  phone's look changes, without the app doing anything. What the app draws the
  phone's way has to be brought up to date by hand, which is the price of the
  second step above.

## What stays the app's own

_Revised in [decision 0067](0067-the-highlight-colours-are-the-owners.md): the
amber below is now the colour the owner chose for the spoken word's mark, made
just dark or light enough to be read on what it is drawn on. The mark is the
same on a light and a dark page, so the page's mark and the app's checks are one
colour in both themes._

- **The colours.** The app keeps its own near-black and off-white, the ones the
  page is set in, rather than the phone's pure black and white. The page being
  read and the app around it are one surface, and an unrelieved black and white
  is what makes a long reading tiring (decision 0022). Pages drawn the phone's
  way use the app's colours in the phone's arrangement: white cards on a light
  grey page, as the phone lays them out, in the app's own greys. The quieter
  words on those pages, a row's value, a header, a footer, are in the phone's
  own grey, which is part of how the phone draws a page rather than one of the
  app's colours.
- **The amber.** Where the phone would colour an action or a check in its own
  blue, the app uses the amber its highlight is painted in, so the one colour
  that means "this is live" is the same everywhere. The one exception is the
  mark on a dark page, which is blue: amber cannot carry the page's light
  letters, and the owner chose to change the mark rather than the app's colour
  (decision 0022). So in the dark the page's mark and the app's checks are two
  colours.
- **The reading page and the player.** They are what the app is for, and the
  phone has no convention for a page whose words light up as they are spoken.
  Decision 0026's references for a quiet reading page, the two reading apps the
  owner used before, still stand for the page. This decision governs everything
  around it.

## What it costs

- **A look of its own.** The app will look like part of the phone rather than
  like a designed product with a character of its own. For an app whose only
  user is its owner, that costs nothing the owner has asked for.
- **Controls the app cannot shape.** When the phone draws a control, the app
  takes it as it comes. The phone's menus carry the phone's own pictures, which
  differ slightly from the app's other icons, and a menu's row goes blank while
  it is open (decision 0035).
- **Copies that fall behind.** A page drawn the phone's way matches the phone it
  was measured on. When the phone's look changes, those pages keep the old look
  until someone measures again.
- **The phone's control is sometimes turned down.** When the phone's own control
  would break a promise the app makes, the app draws its own in the phone's style
  instead. The speed is the example: the phone draws the bubble it opens in, but
  the buttons inside are the app's own, because the phone's stepper would repeat
  at the phone's pace rather than at the pace the reading page can keep up with
  (decision 0041).
- **Consistency with the phone comes before consistency across the app.** The
  settings pages no longer look like the Library, because the phone draws
  settings as cards and a list of things to open as a plain list (decision 0041).

## Where the app does not follow it yet

_Revised in [decision 0066](0066-every-drawer-rises-to-the-drawer-height.md):
the drawers are now the phone's own, in the app's colours._

- **The drawers.** Voice, Contents, Appearance and Download rise in panels of
  the app's own. The phone has panels of its own that do the same, and they are
  not used yet.
- **The icons.** The app's icons are its own set, drawn to match the desktop
  plugin's controls (decision 0026). The phone has its own set. Which of the two
  should win is not decided here.

## Turned down

- **A look of the app's own, applied everywhere.** It would give the app a
  character, but every page would have to be designed from nothing and argued
  about on its own, which is how the settings pages came to be drawn four ways.
- **The phone draws everything.** It would be the closest possible match, and
  the pages would follow the phone's own changes by themselves. But rebuilding
  every page in the phone's own drawing is several times the work of drawing it
  the phone's way, for a difference the owner would barely see (decision 0041).
  Some of what the app needs, the page, the highlight and the player, the phone
  does not offer at all.
