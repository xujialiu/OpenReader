# The app says what it is made of

## What was decided

At the foot of Settings, beside Privacy Policy, a row called Acknowledgements
opens a list of everything the app is built from that other people wrote: the
parts it runs on, the parts it plays sound with, the parts it reads a book
with. Each line names the part and the terms it was given under, and a tap
shows those terms in full, in the words their authors wrote. There are about a
hundred and thirty of them.

The list is made from the app itself each time the parts change, not written
by hand. A part that arrives hidden inside another is listed as well, under
its own name.

## Why

Nearly everything in the app beyond its own screens is other people's work,
given away on one condition: that the notice saying so goes with every copy.
While the app lived only on its author's phone, nothing was being handed to
anyone. Once it is in the store, every download is a copy, and the notices
have to travel with it.

A list made by hand would be wrong within a month. Parts are added and updated
as the app changes, and the ones hidden inside other parts do not show up
anywhere a person would look. So the list is made from what the app actually
contains, and the app's own checks fail when a part changes and the list has
not been made again.

## Alternatives turned down

- **The notices on the project's website, linked from the app.** Less to build,
  and the list would not lengthen Settings. But the notices would then be
  somewhere else, not in the copy, which is the one thing the terms ask. The
  owner chose to have them inside the app.
- **One long page with every notice on it.** The phone's own acknowledgements
  are a list of names, each opening its own text. A single page of legal text
  a hundred and thirty notices long is one nobody could find their way around.
- **Only the parts the author chose directly.** Most of the list is parts that
  those parts brought with them. The terms apply to them just the same.

## What it costs

- **A second row at the foot of Settings.** It is where the phone puts its own
  legal rows, below everything the owner uses day to day.
- **A long list of small print.** It is there for whoever wants to know what
  the app is made of. Nobody is asked to read it.
- **A step whenever the parts change.** Making the list again is one command,
  and the app's checks refuse a change that skips it.
