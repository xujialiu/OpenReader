## The three font "kinds" did change a Chinese book, and issue #12 said they did not (2026-09-21 00:18)

Issue #12 was written claiming `READING_FONTS`' three entries do nothing on a Chinese book, because Georgia, Times New Roman and Helvetica carry no Chinese glyphs. That is false, and `highlighter.ts`'s own comment above the list already said so: WebKit resolves `font-family` **per script**, so the generic family at the end of each stack is what a Chinese run gets. `Georgia, "Times New Roman", serif` sets Latin in Georgia and Chinese in the system's serif CJK face, in the same paragraph — and `Helvetica, Arial, sans-serif` gives it a modern one instead. The two kinds were already different on a Chinese novel.

`test/renderer/appearance.test.ts` enforced two rules: every stack ends in `serif` or `sans-serif`, and no stack matches `/[一-鿿]|PingFang|Songti|Heiti/`. Reading the reasoning rather than the assertion showed the two were not the same claim. The danger described — "a font that has no glyphs for it" — is a stack that *terminates* at a Latin face, and `"Songti SC", serif` does not: it wins where it has glyphs and keeps the generic behind it. So the first rule stays and the second goes (ADR 0029), which is what lets the list name 宋体 and 楷体 at all.

The correction cost nothing to find and would have cost a wrong feature to miss: acting on the issue as written would have replaced the kinds with six Latin faces that change nothing for the books this app was built to read.

## Three of four CJK faces are absent from the iOS 27.0 simulator runtime (2026-09-21 01:32)

`READING_FONTS` held 苹方, 宋体, 楷体 and 圆体 for one commit. On the iPhone 17 / iOS 27.0 simulator, `UIFont.familyNames` contains **PingFang (SC/TC/HK/MO) and no other CJK family at all** — `Songti SC`, `Kaiti SC` and `Yuanti SC` are simply not there. The ios-tester established it twice in one run: the font registry directly, and a screenshot of the Fonts page in which the four Chinese rows are visually indistinguishable from each other and from System.

Selecting 楷体 left 仙逆's text unchanged. That was established by diffing identical crops rather than by eye, which matters — the same method showed `Times New Roman` and `Original Book Font` rendering **pixel-identically** on the English fixture, because that EPUB carries no CSS of its own and WebKit's bare default already renders as Times. Two serif faces are not something to compare by looking.

Nothing broke: an absent face falls through to its generic, which is what the stacks are built for. But the rows were inert, and the owner's call was to take all four out rather than keep the one that worked. ADR 0029 records what would have to be true to put them back, and that a physical iPhone ships a fuller font set than a simulator runtime is the obvious first thing to check.

The measurement to keep: **a font named in `READING_FONTS` can be absent and nothing says so.** There is no error, no warning and no visible difference from the system font — the row simply stops doing anything. Anything added to that list is worth checking on a device before it is believed.
